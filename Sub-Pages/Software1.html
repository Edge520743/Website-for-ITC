About Information Systems

To define Information systems, we need to break it down further into two things, data and information.

Data is defined as the raw observation of numbers, and statistics which has yet to be defined (or added context).

There are two types of data:

  • Empirical: Observable data that contains factual, and verifiable information.
  • Non-empirical: Non-observable data that is interpreted from a fictional source.

Information is defined as the collected, and organized sets of data that are defined (or have context). It can be the following:

  • Information as process: When a person is informed about a change, or when the information in general has been changed.
  • Information as knowledge: Information that is known taken from the gathered information through “information as process”. This type of information is purely conceptual, meaning it has no physical form.
  • Information as thing: Type of information that is based from an object (i.e. data).

The Knowledge Hierarchy System

Data and Information are actually part of something known as the “knowledge hierarchy system”. Russell Ackoff created the knowledge hierarchy system and it shows the progression from data to wisdom.

Rusell's Knowledge Hierarchy Triangle

  • Wisdom: At the top since it is defined to be the capacity of effectiveness, thus wisdom has the ability to use judgement.
  • Intelligence: Second to wisdom, defined to be the capacity for efficiency.
  • Understanding, Knowledge, & Information: Both Understanding, Knowledge, and Information helps increase efficiency in understanding the source of information.
  • Data: At the bottom as it is defined to be just a representation of statistics. The difference between information and data is purely functional.

According to Dammann (2019), Ackmann’s hierarchy can be heavily simplified into DIEK (Data, Information, Evidence, Knowledge).

Dammann's Knowledge Hierarchy Triangle

  • Data: Gathered from observations that are meant to be used raw, or without context.
  • Information: Made from the creation of context with data, and used for curation purposes.
  • Evidence: Created from comparison, and this comparison is used for decision making.
  • Knowledge: Its goal is for justification made based on a consensus.

What is Information Systems?

Information Systems (IS) are systems that collects information, provide knowledge, and then disseminate the data as output for the user(s) to use. In general, IS refers to a system of components, including the human as well. This means that IS does not focus only for computers, but the human processing aspect as well.

IS utilizes a process known as ”data processing”. Data processing refers to a process that summarizes a user's (or various user’s) input into a proper, reported output. IS uses various processes in order to process and decide the data:

  • Aggregation
  • Validity (quality of said data)
  • Sufficiency
  • Availability

Factors of Success in Information Systems

While IS is important, having a bad IS system can cause various issues in your prior static system. Therefore, in order to create a successful IS system, there are several factors to be reminded of:

  • System Quality
  • Information Quality
  • Use
  • User Satisfaction
  • Individual Impact
  • Organizational Impact

Information Systems vs Information Techonology

(In general, IT creates a static system with set rules while IS helps add and update those rules in order to keep it up-to-date.)

    Innformation Systems

  • IS is the combination of IT and the use of informal rules (rules not defined by the prior formal rules), thus making it a dynamic system that allows change.

    Information Techonlogy

  • Information Technology (IT) is about the formal rules created prior on how data is stored, thus making it a static system.

The Different Types of Process Systems in Information Systems

Transaction Process System

A type of system that prioritizes routine, everyday activities.

Management Information System

A type of system that helps provide information regarding regular operations in order for a decision to be made.

Knowledge Information System

Type of system that contains data that is not purely numerical. In general, this system is used for situations that numerical methods cannot solve.

Decision Support System

Type of system that assist in the decision making process, can either be unstructured or structured: Structured is the data that is well-organized and can be inputted into a database system. Unstructured is the unorganized data that was not formatted prior.

Group Decision Support System

Another type of the DSS system that in order to reach a proper conclusion with a group of people.

Executive Support System

A type of system specialized for high-level ranks in the company. Its main purpose is to decide critical factors in a business.

Analysis

Based on the information I have gathered, I have realized that data and information is just a part of something bigger known as the “knowledge hierarchy”. This hierarchy details clearly how wisdom is processed into data, and is considered to be the core process in information systems. The reason for this is that data, information, knowledge, wisdom, etc. can be interpreted as “inputs” to be stored inside an information system. Additionally, these “inputs” are processed into reported outputs, which can either be structured (formatted) or unstructured (unformatted). Thus, it can be said that without the knowledge hierarchy, information systems in general would cease to function.

Moreover, I have discovered that the “knowledge hierarchy” can be simplified further into the “DIEK” hierarchy by Dammann. This hierarchy still is detailed enough, but caters more to the modern context of today’s world. Additionally, having just the “knowledge hierarchy” part of your information system does not mean immediately that it will become successful. There are factors to this success that must be thought of first before thinking about the knowledge hierarchy. The factors in general pertain to both user usability, user satisfaction, sustainability, and maintenance, therefore creating a system in general requires the thought of both the “knowledge hierarchy” and the factors of success, which will increase the system’s stability and uses.

Furthermore, in terms of the stability of an information system, I have discovered that there is a difference between IT (Information Technology) and IS (Information Systems). IT pertains to the static, formal rules of the created system in order to fulfill a goal, whereas IS is about the informal, dynamic rules that bend the formal rules of the system in order to satisfy a need (i.e., updating the outdated information).

In addition, I have further improved my knowledge on regarding support systems (within the context of information systems). In general, the use cases of each support system depends, either on the type of user or the day-by-day activities required. Transaction process systems are used for routine activities, management information systems are used for business operations, knowledge information systems are utilized for information that require context, decision support system, group decision support system, and the executive support system are used for decision making in general, catering to specific people or groups of people. Therefore, choosing the right type of support system will allow your information system to further increase its chance of success.

In conclusion, I now have a better understanding of what information, data, information systems, and its support systems are in general. Without the knowledge hierarchy, there would be no information systems, and without information systems, support systems would cease to exist in general. Therefore, every part of the information system is crucial to its function as a system.
