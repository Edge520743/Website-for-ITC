Explore, learn, and get confident with technology right here, and right now. Computers are interesting things. At their core, they are electronic devices that take inputs, process them, and produce outputs. Yet with that single principle, computers can do various things such as playing videos and games, solving mathematical equations, tackling complex problems humanity has not solved yet, and connecting people via the internet.

So what are… Computers?

In general, anything that can take an input and output can be considered as a computer, however in this day and age, we usually refer to computers as devices that process, store, and display information (Britannica 1). With this definition in mind, computers can range from embedded systems as small as a bug to NASA-grade supercomputers that can fit an entire office space. Basically, so long as something takes an input, processes said input, then produces the output, then that is a modern-day computer.

While we depend on computers in our daily lives, we should take note that they are never perfect. Computers have theoretical limitations, one of the most famous ones is the “Halting Problem” wherein a computer program cannot predict when itself will stop, thus causing an infinite loop (Britannica 2). Think of it as plugging in a socket to itself. The electricity forever loops and has nowhere to go, similar to how a program when predicting itself cannot go anywhere until it is told to stop. This theoretical limit is a core mechanic to modern day computing since it defines the limitation of computers (like how a math function has limits) (GeeksforGeeks 1). Thus, the “Halting Problem” revolutionized mathematics in general, further confirming that certain propositions cannot be true, even if you had some way to automate it.
